1. Title & Problem Definition

Innov CRM: Streamlining B2B Sales Cycles by Eliminating Manual SLA Dispatch Gaps and Multi-User Stage Collisions

Context & Background

Innov CRM is a dedicated B2B Sales Workflow system designed to accelerate pipelines for high-growth mid-market SaaS teams. As the lead Product Manager, I owned the opportunity workspace and pipeline progression engine.

Crucially, this product required tight cross-functional alignment. I acted as the core integration bridge, directly managing and collaborating with:

  • The Co-Founder: Aligned the visual pipeline release roadmap with strict investor seed-funding timelines and product pitch sessions.
  • Marketing Lead: Integrated webhook telemetry between inbound lead capture forms (WordPress/Webflow) and the CRM dispatch engine.
  • Sales Team: Shadowed 8 active account executives (AEs) and sales development reps (SDRs) daily to diagnose operational pipeline friction.
  • Engineering Stakeholders: Partnered with backend and frontend developers to design low-latency client-side state synchronization.

Problem Statement

Sales agents spent 2.5 to 3 hours daily manually updating opportunity stages, tracking SLA follow-ups in external sheets, and resolving database race conflicts when multiple reps attempted to edit or claim the exact same inbound deals simultaneously.

How might we design a rapid-action visual workspace that automates compliance tasks and guarantees transactional integrity under heavy concurrent usage?

Stakeholder Management: Influencing Without Authority

Navigating conflicting goals across different teams was a daily operational reality. I addressed stakeholder friction through a series of key decisions:

  • The Co-Founder (Scope Conflict): The co-founder pushed to bundle a custom email sequence builder into the MVP. I presented engineering estimates proving it would delay our public release by 6 weeks, clashing with our seed pitch timeline. I secured approval to cut this and focus solely on the kanban drag-and-drop workspace, ensuring a timely release.
  • Marketing Lead (Qualification Conflict): Marketing requested a mandatory 12-field lead qualification form for all inbound traffic to protect lead quality. I ran a quick 7-day conversion audit proving that forms with more than 4 fields suffered a 28% sign-up drop-off. By showing the hard data, I influenced Marketing to defer detailed questions to a post-signup onboarding wizard.
  • Sales Representatives (Friction Auditing): I closed the trust loop with our sales agents by running weekly feedback sprints. Instead of delivering heavy monthly launches, I shipped minor layout improvements every Friday, driving rapid internal adoption of the CRM pipeline.

To manage our feature backlog against our tight launch deadline, I applied the RICE Framework (Reach, Impact, Confidence, Effort) to evaluate scope candidates. Below is the scorecard that guided our engineering sprints:

Solution & Reasoning

Our solution comprised a real-time Kanban board with an event-driven task listener, paired with client-side reactive rendering to eliminate pipeline delay.

To solve write clashes without locking threads or slowing down the database, we chose an optimistic locking schema. Every deal card database record has a version_id. When an agent drags a card, the client sends the current version ID. If a write conflict is detected (because another agent updated the record first), the system gracefully prompts the agent to refresh, preserving data integrity without database deadlocks.

Go-to-Market (GTM) Launch & Rollout

To protect user data and operational integrity, I designed a structured 3-phase rollout plan:

  • Phase 1: Internal Sandbox Alpha (1 Week): Released to 3 selected sales representatives to stresstest the optimistic locking scheme and audit for database write clashes under concurrent campaigns.
  • Phase 2: Partner Beta Launch (2 Weeks): Expanded to 2 mid-market SaaS sales teams (35 active agents). Delivered targeted sales enablement guides and established a dedicated Slack feedback loop to monitor pipeline velocity and UI friction.
  • Phase 3: GA (General Availability): Self-serve onboarding and interactive UI tutorials rolled out to all teams, achieving seamless adoption.

Learnings & Tradeoffs

Optimistic locking was chosen over pessimistic locking because sales reps rarely edit the exact same deal card at the exact same millisecond. Choosing optimistic locking avoided database thread blockage and preserved rapid write speeds.

The Tradeoff: Setting atomic database lock versions protects write integrity but adds minor server overhead during mass campaign list imports. As a PM, this proved that protecting data consistency is a non-negotiable bedrock for sales teams.

1. Title & Problem Definition

ModelMetrics: Building an Interactive PCA Telemetry Dashboard to Simplify High-Value Customer Segmentation

Context & Background

Growth marketing teams need to divide users into segments to run targeted campaigns. In most companies, this requires writing complex SQL queries or asking data engineers to run localized Python notebooks.

Problem Statement

How might we give non-technical marketing leads a visual tool to explore multi-dimensional customer segments without waiting for manual CSV exports?

Research & Insights

Shadowing data scientists and growth leads revealed a clear bottleneck:

  • Slow Turnaround Times: 80% of marketing campaigns were delayed because teams had to wait up to a week for data analysts to extract cohort segments.
  • Difficult Visualizations: Standard dashboards showed static charts but did not let users interact with multi-dimensional datasets directly.

Solution & Reasoning

We built an interactive 2D scatter plot interface representing Principal Component Analysis (PCA) clustering. Marketers can draw bounding boxes directly over visual clusters to isolate specific cohorts. A webhook action then pushes the selected cohort directly to marketing tools.

We chose to render the PCA chart on the client-side using lightweight Canvas drawing rather than SVG nodes. This maintained a fluid 60fps frame rate even when loading tens of thousands of data points, though it meant losing built-in CSS transitions on individual nodes.

1. Title & Problem Definition

MarketingOS: Consolidating fragmented ad campaign cohorts and tracking real-time Lead Response Time

Context & Background

Performance marketing managers rely on multiple ad networks to acquire customers. They have to log into separate platforms to measure daily conversions, making optimization slow and manual.

Problem Statement

How might we unify ad campaign analytics into a single dashboard to track cohort performance and Lead Response Time in real-time?

Research & Insights

Interviews with growth leads highlighted two major inefficiencies:

  • Manual Overhead: Marketing specialists spent an average of 3.5 hours every morning copying and pasting campaign data into Excel sheets.
  • Ad Spend Leakage: Fragmented data delayed campaign optimization, resulting in up to 15% wasted budget on non-converting ad groups.

Solution & Reasoning

We built a multi-tenant workspace analytics dashboard that aggregates conversion metrics and tracks Lead Response Time (LRT) in real-time. By connecting data pipelines directly to active ad networks, we removed the need for manual reports.

1. Title & Problem Definition

researchOS: Moving scholarly searches from raw keywords to context-aware semantic space matching

Context & Background

Academic researchers query thousands of papers. Traditional keyword search systems frequently miss relevant work because they do not understand the conceptual context behind different terms.

Problem Statement

How might we build a semantic search system that retrieves contextually relevant research papers and reduces empty results?

Research & Insights

Analyzing user queries and results revealed clear search barriers:

  • High Rate of Empty Results: 31% of academic queries returned no results because users wrote descriptive questions instead of exact matching terms.
  • Fragmented Terminology: Search engines missed up to half of relevant papers because authors used different terminologies for the same core concepts.

Solution & Reasoning

We built a semantic RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation) search pipeline. It translates user queries into vector space embeddings and searches papers based on conceptual distance, solving the limitations of keyword matching.

We selected a smaller, optimized sentence-transformer embedding model running locally rather than a cloud-hosted API. This reduced network latency from 1.2s to 450ms, though it slightly reduced retrieval accuracy on highly specialized biological papers.
